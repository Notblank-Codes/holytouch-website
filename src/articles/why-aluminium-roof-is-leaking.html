Most leaks on aluminium roofs come from the joints and the fixings, not from the middle of a sheet: loose or rusted screws, side laps that don't close, ridge caps that are too narrow, failed flashing where the roof meets a wall, and blocked gutters. The stain on your ceiling is often a metre or more away from where the water actually gets in.

Anyone who has had a leaking roof knows the routine: stained ceiling, damp walls, buckets on the floor every time it rains. Most leaks are not a mystery, though. Once the real cause is found, they can be fixed.

The common causes

1. Loose, missing or rusted screws

Every screw is a hole in the roof. If the washer is missing, the screw is over-tightened and crushed the sheet, or it has worked loose as the wood dried and shrank, water gets in around it. This is the single most common cause we find.

2. Side laps that don't close

Where one sheet overlaps the next, the ribs must sit snugly on top of each other. If the sheets were laid out of line, or a sheet was bent during fixing, a gap opens and rain blows in. On step tile and metcopo sheets, some roofers double the lap for extra protection, though that uses roughly 8–12% more sheet.

3. Ridge cap too narrow or badly fixed

If the ridge cap doesn't cover enough of the sheets on both sides, wind-driven rain gets under it. The same goes for hip caps on a hip roof.

4. Flashing where the roof meets a wall

Parapet walls, walls of a higher building and water tanks all need flashing. When the flashing is missing, cracked or just stuck on with sealant, water runs down the wall and into the roof.

5. Tiny holes from a faulty machine

When a sheet-forming machine isn't well maintained, it can punch tiny holes in the sheet while pressing it. You may not see them until it rains.

6. Blocked or undersized gutters

If the gutter is full of leaves or too small for heavy rain, water backs up and runs over the fascia and into the roof.

7. A slope that is too low

Very low slopes drain slowly, and water can be pushed back up the laps by the wind. This is a design problem and usually needs more than a patch.

How to find the real source

  1. Mark the stain on the ceiling and measure where it is from two walls.
  2. Go into the roof space on a dry day with a torch. Look for daylight through screw holes, water marks on the wood and rust around fixings.
  3. Follow the water marks uphill. Water runs along rafters and purlins before it drips, so the entry point is usually higher up than the stain.
  4. Check the roof from outside at the ridge, laps, flashing and gutters nearest the stain. Only do this if it's safe; roofs are slippery and fragile.

What a proper repair looks like

  • Changing old screws for new ones with good washers, and filling old holes properly.
  • Re-fixing or replacing sheets with bad laps.
  • Fitting wider ridge or hip caps.
  • Proper flashing tucked into the wall, not just sealant on the surface.
  • Cleaning or resizing gutters.
  • Replacing sheets that are holed or badly dented.

Sealant and felt patches from inside the roof can stop a drip for a while, but they don't fix the cause. If your roof is old, rusted or leaking in many places, re-roofing usually costs less than patching it every rainy season.

Leak questions we hear a lot

Why does my roof only leak in heavy rain?

Usually because wind is pushing water sideways into laps, under ridge caps or behind flashing. Light rain runs straight down and doesn't reach those gaps.

Can a new aluminium roof leak?

Yes, if it was badly fixed: wrong screws, poor laps, narrow ridge caps or no flashing. A new roof should not leak; if it does, call the roofer back early.

Should I repair or replace my roof?

If the leaks are in one or two places and the wood is sound, repair. If the sheets are rusted, thin or leaking in many places, or the wood is rotten, replacing the roof is usually the better use of money.
