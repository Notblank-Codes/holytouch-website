Short answer: for a gable roof, each sheet runs from the edge of the roof to the ridge. Work out that length, then divide the length of the roof by the width one sheet covers. A 15m × 10m bungalow with an 18° slope needs about 48 sheets of 5.9m before any allowance for cutting.

Buying too few sheets stops the job. Buying too many is money sitting on the ground. Here's how roofers work out the quantity, so you can check any list you're given.

What you need to know first

  • Length and width of the building, measured along the outside of the walls.
  • The overhang: how far the roof comes out past the wall. 0.6m is common.
  • The roof slope, in degrees, or the height of the ridge above the wall.
  • The cover width of the sheet: how much width one sheet covers once it overlaps the next. For longspan this is usually a bit under 0.7m.

Step 1: the length of each sheet

Take half the width of the building and add the overhang. That's how far the sheet runs across, flat. Because the roof slopes, the sheet is longer than that. Divide by the cosine of the roof angle (any phone calculator has a "cos" button).

Example: a building 10m wide with a 0.6m overhang and an 18° slope.
Half the width plus overhang = 5m + 0.6m = 5.6m.
5.6 ÷ cos 18° = 5.6 ÷ 0.951 = 5.9m per sheet.

Step 2: the number of sheets on each side

Take the full length of the roof, including the overhang at both ends, and divide by the cover width of one sheet. Round up.

Example: a building 15m long. With 0.6m overhang at both ends, the roof is 16.2m long.
16.2 ÷ 0.686 = 23.6, so 24 sheets per side.

Step 3: total sheets

A gable roof has two sides: 24 × 2 = 48 sheets of 5.9m, or about 283 running metres. Roofers then add an allowance for cutting and mistakes.

Hip roofs are different. The sheets get shorter towards the hips, so it's easier to work in running metres, then add extra for the angled cuts.

Step 4: the accessories

The same measurements give you the rest of the list:

  • Ridge cap: the length of the ridge (plus the hips on a hip roof).
  • Barge board: along the sloping edges at the gable ends.
  • Fascia cladding and eaves angle: along the bottom edges of the roof.
  • Drive screws: on our jobs, about one packet for every 20 metres of sheet.

Common mistakes

  • Using the floor area. The roof is bigger than the floor because of the slope and the overhang.
  • Using the full sheet width instead of the cover width. Sheets overlap, so each one covers less than its full width.
  • Forgetting the overhang at the eaves and the gable ends.
  • Trusting the plan blindly. Buildings don't always match the drawing. Measure the actual walls before you buy.

Questions people ask

How many roofing sheets for a 3-bedroom bungalow?

For a 15m × 10m gable roof at 18° with 0.6m overhang, about 48 longspan sheets of 5.9m, before cutting allowance. Your building may be bigger or smaller, so measure it.

How long is one longspan sheet?

Longspan is cut to order, to the length of your roof slope, so one sheet can run from the eaves all the way to the ridge.

Can I calculate roofing sheets from my building plan?

Yes, use the overall length and width on the plan. But check the measurements on site before buying, and for L-shaped or complex roofs, send us the plan and we'll work it out.
