Price of aluminium roofing sheets in Nigeria: 0.40mm to 0.70mm

What longspan aluminium roofing sheets cost per metre in 2026, how thickness changes the price, and the difference between market and caliper grade.

By HolyTouch Roofing · 21 November 2026 · 3 min read

Aluminium longspan is sold by the running metre, and the price depends mostly on thickness. Our material prices in August 2026 were about ₦7,300 per metre for 0.45mm and ₦9,500 per metre for 0.55mm. Thinner and thicker sheets cost less and more in proportion.

If you've started asking around for roofing sheet prices, you'll have noticed the numbers are all over the place. One person says ₦5,000, another says ₦9,000. Most of that difference comes down to three things: the thickness, the grade, and whether the price includes delivery and fixing.

These are the prices we use in our quotations and in our roof calculator. The 0.45mm and 0.55mm prices come straight from our recent quotations. The others follow the same steps between thicknesses that Lagos factory price lists show.

Per metre or per square metre?

Factories and dealers often quote per square metre. Roofers usually price per running metre of sheet, because the sheets are cut to the length of each slope. The two are not the same, so check which one you've been given before comparing. A longspan sheet covers less than a metre of width once it overlaps the next sheet, so a price per running metre and a price per square metre won't match.

Market grade vs caliper grade

Lagos dealers sell two grades of the same thickness. Market grade is cheaper. Caliper grade (sometimes called full gauge) is closer to the thickness written on it, and costs noticeably more. If one seller's "0.55mm" is far cheaper than another's, ask which grade it is. We explain how to check thickness yourself in how to spot low gauge roofing sheets.

Which thickness should you buy?

  • 0.45mm is fine for longspan on a house if the budget is tight. Some builders avoid 0.45mm for step tile, because the pressed steps are where thin sheets give trouble.
  • 0.55mm is what most of our customers choose for houses. It dents less, it's quieter in heavy rain, and it lasts.
  • 0.60mm and 0.70mm make sense for big halls and warehouses, or where you want the strongest roof you can get.

Don't forget the accessories

The sheets are only part of the bill. A full roof also needs ridge cap, barge board, fascia cladding, eaves angle, wall flashing, screws, seam bolts, rivets, silicone and flash band. On a typical bungalow, these add a good amount on top of the sheets. Our roofing cost guide shows full totals by house size.
